How to actually get good at this

Read this before you open Sheet 01. Five minutes now saves you months of unfocused grinding.

Note 01 — Patterns beat problems

There are thousands of problems catalogued online and roughly twenty real patterns behind them. Grind problems at random and you're memorizing thousands of individual answers. Learn patterns first, and every new problem you meet is just "this is Sliding Window with an extra twist" — you half-know the solution before you finish reading it. That's why this manual is organized by pattern, not by problem number, and why every sheet opens with Field Notes before a single problem link. Read that section slowly — it's the actual lesson; the problems are just reps.

Note 02 — The four-step loop

Run this same loop on every problem, forever. It's the whole method.

  • 1. Understand. Restate the problem in your own words. Work the given examples by hand. Name the edge cases (empty input, one element, duplicates, negatives) before writing anything.
  • 2. Match the pattern. Use the Recognition Marks on the sheet — does this smell like a window, a graph, a DP state? You get faster at this with every sheet you complete.
  • 3. Brute force first, then optimize. Get a correct, even if slow, solution in your head or on paper. The repeated work in that brute force is exactly what the pattern eliminates.
  • 4. Code it, then break it. Write the solution, then deliberately try to break it with edge cases. This is what actually builds the instinct interviewers are testing for.
Note 03 — The 25-minute rule

Give every problem a real, focused 20–25 minutes before you look at the video walkthrough or a hint. Struggling productively is where the learning happens — peek too early and you'll recognize the solution on sight but won't be able to produce it yourself next time. Still stuck after 25 minutes? Watch the video, understand why that approach works, close it, and re-type the solution from memory. Then come back and re-solve the same problem cold in 3–5 days — that gap is what turns short-term recognition into long-term recall.

Note 04 — Suggested pace

Consistency beats intensity. A sustainable pace gets most learners through the full manual in about 2–3 months:

Volume I — Foundations~2 weeks · 3–4 problems/day
Volume II — Core Patterns~4 weeks · 3 problems/day
Volume III — Mastery~4 weeks · 2–3 problems/day, denser
Every SundayNo new problems — re-solve 2 old ones cold
Note 05 — When you're really stuck
  • Re-read the Field Notes and Recognition Marks on the sheet — the answer is usually a sentence you skimmed past.
  • Work a tiny example by hand (n=2 or n=3) and trace exactly what should happen, step by step.
  • Ask "what if I already knew the answer for a smaller version of this input?" — that question is the seed of every DP and recursive solution.
  • It's fine to watch the video. It is not fine to skip re-solving the problem yourself afterward.
